# Comparing Hinge Quotations Against the Same Specification

Before comparing prices, check that each quotation refers to the same part and drawing revision. Two offers described as “weld-on hinges” may cover different constructions or dimensions.

## Match the product reference

Keep the family, exact size or model and drawing revision in the comparison. If a supplier proposes an alternative, put it in a separate row and identify what differs. A substitute should not disappear into the price column as though it were the requested part.

List open technical questions alongside the offer. An unanswered detail remains open even if the quotation contains a price.

## Put commercial terms in separate fields

Record the currency, quantity basis, unit basis, destination and any shipping or packaging terms explicitly stated in the offer. Ask about missing fields instead of assuming that two suppliers use the same basis.

Where a quotation gives a validity period or delivery commitment, retain its exact wording and conditions. Do not replace it with a general statement about the supplier.

## Keep attachment revisions under control

Save the quoted drawing with the quotation. If you send a revised drawing later, ask which parts of the offer need to be updated. An email conversation with several drawings does not make it clear which one a price covers.

## A compact comparison record

Use one row per proposed product and include: supplier reference, buyer drawing revision, exact size, quantity, quoted unit, currency, stated terms and unresolved questions.

Keep private pricing and customer drawings inside your own procurement records. If preparing a public example, use an empty template or obtain permission to use an anonymized record.
